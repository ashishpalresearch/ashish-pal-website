Computer vision for infrastructure monitoring uses images and videos to measure, inspect, and interpret the behaviour and condition of civil infrastructure systems.

This theme is suitable for students interested in image processing, video-based measurement, deep learning, structural dynamics, and AI-assisted infrastructure inspection.

---

## Project Directions

<div class="research-grid">

<div class="research-card">

### Vision-Based Displacement and Vibration Measurement

Develop image/video-based methods to estimate displacement, vibration response, and dynamic properties of structural systems.

**Suitable for:** PhD / M.Tech  
**Methods:** OpenCV, video processing, feature tracking, signal processing, structural dynamics  
**Expected outcomes:** non-contact measurement workflows, validation studies, and publishable computational/experimental results

</div>

<div class="research-card">

### AI-Based Crack and Damage Detection

Develop deep learning and computer vision methods for detecting cracks, visual damage, and deterioration in infrastructure images.

**Suitable for:** PhD / M.Tech / B.Tech  
**Methods:** image processing, segmentation, object detection, deep learning, foundation models  
**Expected outcomes:** damage detection models, benchmark datasets, and automated inspection workflows

</div>

<div class="research-card">

### Video-Based Structural Response Interpretation

Develop methods to convert video measurements into engineering quantities such as displacement, frequency content, mode shapes, and damage-sensitive features.

**Suitable for:** PhD / M.Tech  
**Methods:** computer vision, structural dynamics, modal analysis, signal processing, uncertainty quantification  
**Expected outcomes:** interpretable vision-based SHM tools for structural monitoring

</div>

</div>

## Useful Background

- Python programming
- Image and video processing
- OpenCV
- Machine learning or deep learning
- Structural dynamics
- Signal processing

Students can enter from either civil engineering or computer science-oriented backgrounds, provided they are interested in engineering applications.
