I am actively looking for motivated PhD and M.Tech students interested in mechanics-informed AI, structural health monitoring, digital twins, scientific machine learning, computer vision, sensing technologies, and AI-assisted infrastructure monitoring.

PhD students are expected to pursue long-term research directions, while M.Tech students can take up focused thesis problems with clear research objectives and potential for strong technical contributions. Motivated B.Tech students interested in early research exposure are also encouraged to connect.

The group is being built around the idea of combining **mechanics, sensing, computation, and artificial intelligence** to develop reliable and interpretable tools for civil infrastructure systems.

---

## PhD Positions

I am especially interested in recruiting PhD students who want to work on long-term research problems at the intersection of **structural engineering, mechanics, sensing, scientific computing, and artificial intelligence**.

Potential PhD research directions include:

- mechanics-informed AI for structural health monitoring
- digital twins for civil infrastructure
- physics-informed neural networks for structural dynamics
- interpretable machine learning for nonlinear dynamical systems
- multi-modal sensing and sensor fusion for infrastructure monitoring
- uncertainty-aware condition assessment and decision support
- scientific machine learning for PDE discovery and inverse problems
- computer vision and foundation models for infrastructure inspection

PhD students will be encouraged to develop strong foundations in structural dynamics, computational mechanics, machine learning, scientific computing, and research writing.
The goal is to pursue high-quality research leading to journal publications, open computational tools, and practical methods for intelligent and resilient infrastructure systems.

As the group is growing, early PhD students will have the opportunity to help shape the research culture, computational tools, experimental directions, and long-term identity of the group.

---

## M.Tech Thesis Projects

M.Tech students can work on focused thesis problems that are technically rigorous, research-oriented, and aligned with the broader research directions of the group.

Possible M.Tech thesis areas include:

- camera-based vibration and displacement measurement
- digital twin development for civil structures
- physics-informed learning for structural dynamics
- machine learning for damage detection
- sensor fusion for structural response estimation
- vision-based infrastructure inspection
- finite element modelling combined with AI/ML
- uncertainty quantification for infrastructure monitoring

M.Tech projects will be scoped to match the duration of the thesis while still aiming for meaningful research outcomes, strong technical learning, and, where appropriate, conference or journal publications.

Students interested in continuing toward PhD-level research after the M.Tech thesis are especially welcome.

---


## Why Join the Group?

The group aims to create an environment where PhD students, M.Tech students, and motivated undergraduate students can contribute to research at different levels of depth, with projects scoped according to their academic stage and interests. Students will work on interdisciplinary research problems at the interface of structural engineering, scientific computing, and artificial intelligence.

The research is suitable for students who want to develop strong skills in:

- structural dynamics and structural health monitoring
- scientific computing and finite element modelling
- machine learning and deep learning for engineering systems
- physics-informed and mechanics-guided AI
- computer vision for infrastructure monitoring
- sensor fusion, system identification, and uncertainty quantification
- scientific writing and research publication

The goal is to develop research that can lead to strong academic publications, useful computational tools, and practical applications in resilient infrastructure systems.

## B.Tech Research Projects

Motivated B.Tech students can get involved through B.Tech projects, summer/winter projects, or exploratory research assignments.

Possible entry-level topics include:

- computer vision for crack and damage detection
- Python/MATLAB tools for structural dynamics
- finite element simulations and visualization
- signal processing of vibration data
- literature review and benchmarking of AI methods for civil engineering

B.Tech projects are usually exploratory, but interested students may continue toward M.Tech thesis work or future graduate research.

---

## Expected Background

Useful background areas include:

- structural engineering
- applied mechanics
- structural dynamics
- programming in Python or MATLAB
- machine learning or deep learning
- finite element methods
- signal processing
- numerical methods
- computer vision

Prior experience in all these areas is not required. Students can join with strength in one area and develop the remaining skills during the project.

## How to Apply

Interested students may email with the subject line:

**Prospective PhD/M.Tech/B.Tech Student – [Your Area of Interest]**

Please include:

- CV
- academic transcript
- brief statement of research interests
- relevant project or coding experience
- publications, if any
- whether you are applying through IIT Bombay admissions, external fellowship, or project-based route

In the email, briefly explain which research topic interests you and why.

---

## Note to Prospective Students

You do not need to have prior experience in artificial intelligence or advanced programming before reaching out. A strong interest in research, willingness to learn, and ability to work consistently are the most important qualities.
